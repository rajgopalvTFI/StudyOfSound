Textbook Exercise Questions & Answers from the Balbharati chapter

Only the questions are shown at first. Click on Show Answer to reveal the answer for that question.

  • 1(a). Fill in the blanks and explain.
    Sound does not travel through __________.
    Sound does not travel through vacuum.
  • 1(b). Fill in the blanks and explain.
    The velocity of sound in steel is __________ than the velocity of sound in water.
    The velocity of sound in steel is greater than the velocity of sound in water.
  • 1(c). Fill in the blanks and explain.
    The incidence of __________ in daily life shows that the velocity of sound is less than the velocity of light.
    The incidence of lightning and thunder in daily life shows that the velocity of sound is less than the velocity of light.
  • 1(d). Fill in the blanks and explain.
    To discover a sunken ship or objects deep inside the sea, __________ technology is used.
    To discover a sunken ship or objects deep inside the sea, SONAR technology is used.
  • 2(a). Explain giving scientific reasons.
    The roof of a movie theatre and a conference hall is curved.
    The roof is curved so that sound is heard clearly by people sitting in all parts of the hall. A curved surface reflects sound in such a way that it spreads throughout the hall and the sound energy is distributed more uniformly.
  • 2(b). Explain giving scientific reasons.
    The intensity of reverberation is higher in a closed and empty house.
    In a closed and empty house there are no curtains, furniture or other soft materials to absorb sound. The bare walls, floor and ceiling reflect most of the sound, so the repeated reflections are strong and the intensity of reverberation is higher.
  • 2(c). Explain giving scientific reasons.
    We cannot talk in whispers in a large room like a classroom.
    Whispering produces sound of very low intensity. In a large room this weak sound spreads and becomes even weaker, so it cannot reach students sitting far away. Hence we cannot talk in whispers in a large classroom.
  • 3(a). Answer the following in your own words.
    What are sound absorbing materials? For which purposes are they used?
    Sound absorbing materials are materials that absorb a large part of the sound falling on them instead of reflecting it. They are used to reduce unwanted reflected sound and to decrease reverberation in places such as cinema halls, recording studios and auditoriums.
  • 3(b). Answer the following in your own words.
    Study the relation between density and velocity of sound and answer:
    (i) Which medium has maximum velocity of sound?
    (ii) Which medium has minimum velocity of sound?
    (iii) Why?
    (i) Hydrogen gas has the maximum velocity of sound.
    (ii) Sulphur dioxide gas has the minimum velocity of sound.
    (iii) For a gaseous medium, the velocity of sound is inversely proportional to the square root of density (or molecular weight). So sound travels faster in lighter gases and slower in heavier gases.
  • 3(c). Answer the following in your own words.
    What must be the minimum distance for hearing a distinct echo (at about 22 °C)?
    The minimum distance between the source of sound and the reflecting surface must be about 17.2 m for a distinct echo to be heard at around 22 °C.
  • 3(d). Answer the following in your own words.
    On what factor does the frequency of sound heard by an observer depend?
    The frequency of sound heard by an observer depends on the frequency of vibration of the sound-producing source.
  • 3(e). Answer the following in your own words.
    Explain the structural and functional characteristics of different parts of the ear.
    Our ear has three main parts – outer ear, middle ear and inner ear.

    Outer ear: It consists of the pinna and auditory canal. The pinna collects sound waves and directs them through the canal to the eardrum.

    Middle ear: It contains the eardrum and three small bones – hammer, anvil and stirrup. The eardrum vibrates due to sound waves and these vibrations are transferred and amplified by the three bones.

    Inner ear: It consists of the cochlea and the auditory nerve. The vibrations reaching the cochlea are converted into electrical signals. The auditory nerve carries these signals to the brain, where we experience the sensation of sound.
  • 4. Where and why are sound absorbing materials used?
    Sound absorbing materials are used on the walls, ceiling and floor of places such as cinema halls, theatres, recording studios, conference halls and auditoriums. They absorb a large part of the sound and reduce reverberation so that the sound is clearer and audibility improves.
  • 5(a). The speed of sound in air at 0 °C is 331 m/s. If the speed of sound in air has increased to 346 m/s, find the temperature of air.
    (Given: speed of sound increases by 0.6 m/s for every 1 °C rise in temperature.)
    Increase in speed = 346 − 331 = 15 m/s.
    Every 0.6 m/s increase corresponds to a 1 °C rise in temperature.
    Rise in temperature = 15 ÷ 0.6 = 25 °C.
    Therefore, the temperature of air is 25 °C.
  • 5(b). Nita heard the sound of lightning after 4 s. What was the distance of the lightning from her?
    (Take velocity of sound in air as 340 m/s.)
    Distance = velocity × time = 340 m/s × 4 s = 1360 m.
    So the lightning was 1360 m away from Nita.
  • 5(c). Sunil is standing at a distance of 6 m from a wall. If he shouts loudly, after how much time will he hear the echo?
    (Take velocity of sound in air as 340 m/s.)
    Sound travels from Sunil to the wall and back, so total distance = 2 × 6 = 12 m.
    Time = distance ÷ velocity = 12 ÷ 340 ≈ 0.035 s.
    Sunil will hear the echo after about 0.035 s.
